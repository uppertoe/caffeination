Why baseline p-values mean nothing

When people are allocated at random, any difference between the arms at baseline is chance by construction, so baseline p-values spread evenly between 0 and 1. With five rows in the table, about a fifth of allocations show a “significant” difference somewhere. The CONSORT statement advises against testing baseline differences for this reason.

With only {{ r.n }} colleagues, each test can produce only a limited set of p-values, so the bars are lumpier than the even spread a large trial would show.

Carlisle turned the same fact around. In a 2017 analysis of more than 5,000 trials published in anaesthetic and general medical journals, he looked for baseline tables whose p-values were too evenly matched, or too extreme, to have come from random allocation, and found trials whose data were later shown to be flawed or fabricated.
